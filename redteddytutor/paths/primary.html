A learning example

Hints first. The method, not just the answer.

Try a wrong answer on purpose. Common mistakes get a reply about that mistake, then a hint. “Show me how” opens the worked method.

A demonstration with one fixed question. In the app, questions are generated for your child’s level and every first try is recorded on your device.

Demonstration · Grade 3 maths

Mia has 3 bags with 8 marbles in each. She gives 5 marbles to her brother. How many marbles does Mia have left?

    A similar one, worked through

    Sam has 4 boxes with 6 pencils in each. He loses 3. How many are left?

    1. Find the total first: 4 groups of 6 is 4 × 6 = 24.
    2. “Loses” means take away: 24 − 3 = 21.
    3. Check it makes sense: fewer than 24, so 21 is reasonable.

    Now try Mia’s question the same way.

    What they’ll practise

    The foundations that everything else is built on.

    • Reading

      Phonics, building words and sentences, then short original passages with retrieval, inference and word-choice questions.

    • Spelling & writing

      Spelling in context, vocabulary, word parts and sentence building.

    • Number & calculation

      Place value, the four operations, times tables and two-step word problems.

    • Fractions, decimals & measures

      Comparing fractions, decimals, telling the time, area and perimeter, reading bar charts.

    • Thinking skills

      Patterns, sequences and logic puzzles, each checked to have exactly one answer.

    • Homework help

      Signed-in families can ask Red Teddy for a simpler explanation or another example. It is built to teach, not to do the homework.

    Skills are mapped to related US standards where we are sure of the match, and shown as full or partial coverage on the curriculum page. No state’s own standards have been checked yet.

    How parents and children use it

    A short session most days.

    1. Choose their grade

      A quick check of up to seven questions finds the starting point. No marks are shown.

    2. Today’s plan

      Four activities: a review that is due, a mix-up worth fixing, and new skills whose foundations are in place.

    3. You see what happened

      The parent view shows what was practised, what looks secure and which mistakes keep coming back.
