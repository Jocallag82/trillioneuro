Shapes · Free · No sign-up

Shapes, from posting boxes to sides and corners.

Children learn shapes with their hands first: fitting, turning and posting. Names come next, and only later do they notice what makes a shape a triangle, like three sides and three corners, whichever way up it is.

See 5 activities

What it often looks like by age

Ages 1–2
Fitting a round thing into a round hole, with lots of turning and trying.
Ages 2–3
Matching circles, squares and triangles by outline.
Ages 3–6
Naming shapes, then describing them by sides and corners.

Every child is different, and these are broad patterns, not milestones to test against.

Tips that help

  • Show triangles upside down and stretched too, not only the textbook version.
  • Let your child struggle a little with a shape sorter before helping.
  • Find shapes in real life: plates, windows, pizza slices.

Shapes and matching

Posting box

Ages 1–2Off-screenAbout 10 min

What it builds: Practice matching an object to a space, hand control and cause and effect.

You need

  • A cardboard box with a circle hole cut in the lid
  • Large lids or blocks that fit (too big to swallow)

How to play

  1. Show your child how a lid drops through the hole.
  2. Let them try. Turn the lid to help if they need it.
  3. Open the box together and find everything again.

Things to say

  • In it goes!
  • Where did it go?
  • Round, like a circle.

Take it further

Add a second, square hole once circles are easy.

Shapes

Shapes into holes

Ages 2–3On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 3 min

What it builds: Match circles, squares and triangles by their outline.

You need

  • A shape sorter, or shapes cut from card with matching outlines drawn on paper

How to play

  1. Drag or tap each shape into its hole on screen.
  2. Do the same with card shapes on paper outlines.
  3. Name each shape as it fits.

Things to say

  • Round. That’s a circle.
  • Does it fit? Let’s turn it.
  • The triangle has pointy corners.

Take it further

Go on a circle hunt: plates, clocks, wheels.

Memory and matching

Shape pairs

Ages 2–3On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 3 min

What it builds: Remember where things are and match pairs.

You need

  • Pairs of socks or matching cups

How to play

  1. Play the matching game together, taking turns.
  2. Then hide a toy under one of two cups and swap them slowly.
  3. Ask which cup the toy is under.

Things to say

  • Where was the other circle?
  • You remembered!
  • Which cup is it under?

Take it further

Pair up socks from the laundry.

Shapes

Name the shape

Ages 3–4On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 2 min

What it builds: Name circles, squares, triangles and rectangles.

You need

  • Paper, crayons, scissors (grown-up cuts)

How to play

  1. Play the shapes game together.
  2. Draw shapes and talk about sides and corners.
  3. Make a picture out of cut-out shapes: a house, a rocket.

Things to say

  • How many corners does it have?
  • Is a door a square or a rectangle?
  • What can we make with triangles?

Take it further

Hunt for rectangles around the house.

Geometry

How many sides?

Ages 4–6On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 2 min

What it builds: Name shapes and describe them by their sides and corners.

You need

  • Straws or sticks
  • Play dough balls for corners

How to play

  1. Play the shapes game.
  2. Build a triangle and a square from straws and dough.
  3. Count the sides and corners of each.

Things to say

  • How many sides does a triangle have?
  • What’s the same about a square and a rectangle?
  • What’s different?

Take it further

Build a 3D cube from straws and dough.

Relates to Common Core: K.G.A.2 K.G.B.4
