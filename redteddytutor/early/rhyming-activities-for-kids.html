Rhyming · Free · No sign-up

Rhyming games, from lap rhymes to rhyme chains.

Rhyme helps children hear that words are made of smaller sounds, which is a key step towards reading. It starts with simply enjoying rhymes, then hearing when words rhyme, then making new ones.

See 4 activities

What it often looks like by age

Ages 1–2
Enjoying the rhythm of nursery rhymes and songs.
Ages 3–4
Hearing whether two words rhyme.
Ages 4–6
Making rhymes, including silly made-up words.

Every child is different, and these are broad patterns, not milestones to test against.

Tips that help

  • Silly rhymes count: “bear, chair, flair, zair.”
  • Pause before the rhyming word in a familiar book and let your child fill it in.
  • Make the ending sound a little louder: “c-AT, h-AT”.

Songs and rhymes

Lap rhymes

Ages 1–2Off-screenAbout 5 min

What it builds: Hear the rhythm and repeated sounds of language, which later help with reading.

You need

  • Your voice

How to play

  1. Sit your child on your lap, facing you.
  2. Say “Row, row, row your boat” or “Pat-a-cake”, rocking or clapping along.
  3. Repeat it. Young children love the same rhyme many times.

Things to say

  • Again? Row, row, row…
  • Clap, clap, clap!
  • Where are your hands?

Take it further

Stop just before the last word of a line and let your child fill the gap with a sound or movement.

Rhyming

Which one rhymes?

Ages 3–4On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 3 min

What it builds: Hear when two words end with the same sound.

You need

  • Nursery rhyme book

How to play

  1. Play the rhyming game together.
  2. Read a rhyme and pause before the rhyming word.
  3. Make silly rhymes for family names.

Things to say

  • Cat, hat. They sound the same at the end!
  • Does dog rhyme with log?
  • What rhymes with bear?

Take it further

Make up a two-line rhyme about Red Teddy.

Phonological awareness

Rhyme time

Ages 4–6On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 3 min

What it builds: Recognize and produce rhyming words.

You need

  • None

How to play

  1. Play the rhyming game together.
  2. Say a word and take turns finding rhymes, real or silly.
  3. Read a Dr. Seuss-style rhyming book and pause before the rhyme.

Things to say

  • Cat, bat, hat… what else?
  • Does sun rhyme with run?
  • That’s a silly word, but it rhymes!

Take it further

Make a rhyme chain in the car.

Sequencing and stories

First, next, last

Ages 3–4Off-screenAbout 10 min

What it builds: Retell events in order using first, next and last.

You need

  • A familiar picture book

How to play

  1. After reading, ask what happened first.
  2. Ask what happened next, then at the end.
  3. Use everyday routines too: getting dressed, making toast.

Things to say

  • What happened first?
  • And then?
  • How did it end?

Take it further

Draw three pictures of a story and put them in order.
