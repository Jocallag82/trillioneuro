Colors · Free · No sign-up

Learning colors with toddlers.

Most children understand color words before they can say them, and many mix colors up well into age three. That is normal: color is abstract, because a red cup and a red sock share nothing but their color. Lots of relaxed naming in everyday life does more than drilling.

See 5 activities

What it often looks like by age

Ages 1–2
Hearing color words attached to things: “the red ball”. No need to ask questions yet.
Ages 2–3
Matching same colors, then pointing to a color when asked. Saying color names often comes later.
Ages 3–4
Naming the basic colors, sorting by color and noticing colors in patterns.

Every child is different, and these are broad patterns, not milestones to test against.

Tips that help

  • Say the color with the object: “blue cup”, not just “blue”.
  • Start with one or two colors that look very different, like red and blue.
  • If your child says the wrong color, just say the right one back warmly: “It’s green, like grass.”

Colors

One-color treasure basket

Ages 1–2Off-screenAbout 10 min

What it builds: Notice that different things can share a color, while hearing its name many times.

You need

  • A shallow basket or box
  • 4 to 6 safe household things of one color (too big to swallow)

How to play

  1. Fill the basket with things that are all red: a cup, a sock, a block, a ball.
  2. Let your child take things out and put them back.
  3. Name each one with its color: “A red sock.”
  4. Next week, try a different color.

Things to say

  • Red cup. Red ball.
  • Can you give me the red one?
  • Oh, it goes back in the basket!

Take it further

On a walk, stop and point to anything red you see.

First words

Look and Name cards

Ages 1–2On-screen, togetherAbout 2 min

What it builds: Hear and connect everyday words to pictures: colors, animals and familiar things.

You need

  • A phone or tablet, on your lap together

How to play

  1. Open the cards and hold the screen where you can both see it.
  2. Point to the picture and name it slowly: “A duck!”
  3. Tap the card to hear the word and the sound, then wait for your child to look, point or babble.
  4. After six cards, put the screen away and find one of the things for real.

Things to say

  • Where is the duck?
  • What does the cow say? Moo!
  • Your ball is red too.

Take it further

Find one thing from the cards in your home and give it to your child to hold.

Colors

Find the color

Ages 2–3On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 2 min

What it builds: Recognize and name the basic colors.

You need

  • Colored socks, blocks or toys

How to play

  1. Play the short on-screen game together.
  2. Then lay out three colored socks and ask for one by color.
  3. Swap roles: your child asks you for a color.

Things to say

  • Can you find the blue one?
  • Yes, blue like the sky.
  • Which one is yellow?

Take it further

Sort laundry by color into piles.

Sorting

Tidy-up sorting

Ages 2–3Off-screenAbout 10 min

What it builds: Group things by one feature: kind, color or size.

You need

  • Toys to put away
  • Two boxes

How to play

  1. Choose one rule: cars in this box, animals in that box.
  2. Hand over toys one by one and ask where each goes.
  3. Next time, sort by color or by big and small.

Things to say

  • Is this a car or an animal?
  • Big ones here, small ones there.
  • You sorted them all!

Take it further

Sort spoons and forks when unloading the dishwasher (safe items only).

Memory

Color pairs

Ages 3–4On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 3 min

What it builds: Hold where things are in mind and find matching pairs.

You need

  • A tray and five household objects
  • A tea towel

How to play

  1. Play the color pairs game together.
  2. Put five objects on a tray, cover them and remove one.
  3. Ask which one is missing.

Things to say

  • Where was the other green one?
  • What’s missing?
  • How did you remember?

Take it further

Make it six objects when five is easy.
