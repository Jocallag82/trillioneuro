Red Teddy’s early-years games

Four learning journeys for ages 1 to 6. Short, calm games with big pictures, Red Teddy’s gentle help, and an idea to carry on away from the screen. No timers, no scores, nothing recorded.

Ages 1–2

Little World

Look, listen, name and point together. Big pictures, no timers, no wrong answers. 10 activities, easiest first. Off-screen ideas for ages 1–2.

For grown-ups

  • Sit together and hold the screen between you. Name what you see.
  • Short is best: one or two games, then play with the real things.
  • There are no wrong answers here. A wrong tap simply shows the right picture.
  • If your child loses interest, stop. Pointing, babbling and copying sounds all count.
  • Tap and hear · Animals and sounds

    Meet the Animals

    Hear the names of familiar animals and link each one to its sound.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Listening, looking, and connecting a word and a sound to a real animal.
    Play together
    Make the sound together, then wait. Many little ones try to copy.
    Off-screen
    Look for a dog, a bird or a cat on your next walk and make its sound together.
  • Tap and hear · Everyday words

    Everyday Words

    Hear the names of objects your child sees every day.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Building first vocabulary around the home.
    Play together
    Say what each thing is for: “Cup. We drink from a cup.”
    Off-screen
    At bath or meal time, name three things your child can touch.
  • Off-screen, together · Off-screen

    Talk Together

    Short, shared activities with real objects at home.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Conversation, turn-taking and new words, the most powerful learning at this age.
    Play together
    Follow your child’s lead. Copy their sounds and add one word.
    Off-screen
    Choose any idea below.
  • Find it · Everyday words

    Find the Object

    Pick out a named, familiar object from two large pictures.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Understanding a word before being able to say it (receptive language).
    Play together
    Name both pictures first, then ask. Pointing counts as an answer.
    Off-screen
    Put a cup and a spoon on the table and ask “Where’s the cup?”
  • Tap and hear · Animals and sounds

    Animal Sounds

    Hear an animal’s sound first, then its name.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Copying sounds: an early step towards speech.
    Play together
    Make the sound before tapping, then check together.
    Off-screen
    Sing “Old MacDonald” and pause so your child can make the sound.
  • Tap and hear · Colors

    Colors Around Us

    Hear color names paired with real things that are that color.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Noticing color. Naming colors usually comes much later, and that is fine.
    Play together
    Point to something nearby that is the same color.
    Off-screen
    Find three red things around your home and line them up.
  • Memory · Memory

    Peekaboo Memory

    See pictures hide and find them again.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Remembering where something went (object permanence and early memory).
    Play together
    Say “Peekaboo!” as each card turns over. Help with the second card.
    Off-screen
    Hide a toy under a cloth while your child watches, then find it together.
  • Find it · Listening

    Listen and Find

    Hear a word, without seeing it written, and find the matching picture.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Listening carefully and linking a spoken word to a picture.
    Play together
    Say the word with Red Teddy, slowly and clearly.
    Off-screen
    Play “Can you bring me…?” with two toys on the floor.
  • Find it · Comparing

    Big and Small

    Notice which of two pictures is big and which is small.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    An early comparison word, shown in the clearest possible way.
    Play together
    Stretch your arms wide for “big”, then make your hands tiny for “small”.
    Off-screen
    Put a big spoon and a small spoon side by side. Which is big?
  • Find it · Listening

    What’s That Sound?

    Match a sound to a familiar animal or object.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Careful listening and remembering sounds.
    Play together
    Make the sound yourself and see if your child looks at the right picture.
    Off-screen
    Close your eyes together and listen: can you hear a car, a bird, a clock?

Ages 2–3

Explore and Discover

Matching, sorting, colors, shapes and sounds with real-world pictures. 12 activities, easiest first. Off-screen ideas for ages 2–3.

For grown-ups

  • Name every picture before your child chooses.
  • Simplify: cover one choice with your hand so there are only two.
  • Stretch: ask “Why?” after each answer.
  • Hints come after a miss; the answer is shown kindly after three tries.
  • Match pairs · Matching

    Snap! Match the Same

    Match two identical photographs.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Looking closely and noticing what is the same.
    Play together
    Name each pair as it is matched: “Two cats!”
    Off-screen
    Pair up socks from the laundry together.
  • Tap and hear · Vocabulary

    Word Cards

    Learn new words for things in the wider world.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Vocabulary beyond the home: vehicles, nature and animals.
    Play together
    After each word, ask “Have we seen one of these?”
    Off-screen
    On a walk, count how many things from the cards you spot.
  • Sort · Sorting

    Tidy Up Sort

    Sort familiar things into two simple groups.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Grouping by what things are (classification).
    Play together
    Ask “Can we eat it?” to help decide.
    Off-screen
    At tidy-up time, sort toys: cars in one box, blocks in another.
  • Find it · Colors

    Find the Color

    Find a picture of a named color.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Matching color words to real things.
    Play together
    Name the color of every picture first.
    Off-screen
    I spy something… yellow! Take turns finding colors in the room.
  • Match pairs · Animals

    Mommies and Babies

    Match animal parents to their babies.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Noticing likeness and learning baby-animal words.
    Play together
    Teach the baby names: puppy, kitten, lamb, calf.
    Off-screen
    Look at family photos: who is the grown-up, who is the baby?
  • Find it · Shapes

    Shape Hunt

    Recognize circles, squares, triangles and stars.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Shape names and noticing corners and curves.
    Play together
    Trace each shape in the air with a finger.
    Off-screen
    Find a circle at home: a plate, a clock, a lid.
  • Find it · Listening

    Sound Match

    Match a familiar sound to the picture that makes it.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Listening and telling sounds apart.
    Play together
    Copy the sound together before choosing.
    Off-screen
    Tap a spoon on a pot, then on a cup. Which sounds louder?
  • Find it · Everyday words

    Kitchen and Home

    Recognize familiar foods and household objects by their use.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Knowing what everyday things are and what they are for.
    Play together
    Talk about what we do with each thing.
    Off-screen
    At snack time, name every food on the plate.
  • Find it · Language

    What Am I?

    Find an everyday object from a simple spoken clue.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Understanding descriptions and what things are used for.
    Play together
    Act out the clue: pretend to drink, sleep or kick.
    Off-screen
    Give a clue about something in the room: “It’s soft and you sleep on it.”
  • Find it · Patterns

    What Comes Next?

    Continue a simple two-item pattern.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Spotting repeats: a building block of math.
    Play together
    Chant it: “apple, banana, apple, banana…”
    Off-screen
    Clap, stamp, clap, stamp. What comes next?
  • Find it · Concepts

    Opposites

    Explore big/small, up/down, full/empty and open/closed.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Pairs of describing words that help children compare.
    Play together
    Act each one out: reach up high, crouch down low.
    Off-screen
    At bath time: full cup, empty cup. Pour and say it.
  • Find it · Puzzles

    Picture Puzzles

    Complete a picture by finding its missing half.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Seeing how parts make a whole.
    Play together
    Ask “What is this a picture of?” before choosing.
    Off-screen
    Cut an old card in two and put it back together.

Ages 3–4

Learn Through Play

Counting, first letter sounds, patterns, memory, rhymes and short stories. 15 activities, easiest first. Off-screen ideas for ages 3–4.

For grown-ups

  • Count, clap and say sounds out loud together.
  • Simplify: do one round, then play the off-screen idea.
  • Stretch: ask your child to explain or to be the teacher.
  • Mistakes are part of learning. Nothing is scored or recorded.
  • Touch and count · Counting

    Count to 5

    Count a group of 1 to 5 objects, touching each one once.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    One-to-one counting and knowing that the last number says how many.
    Play together
    Count out loud together and touch each one as you say a number.
    Off-screen
    Count toys into a box: one, two, three…
  • Match pairs · Shapes

    Shape Pairs

    Match shapes that are the same, even when color and size differ.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Seeing shape regardless of color: an important step in classification.
    Play together
    Count corners together: a triangle has three.
    Off-screen
    Draw shapes in chalk outside and jump into the one you name.
  • Match pairs · Numbers

    Number Match

    Match the numerals 1 to 6 to groups of dots.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Linking a written number to the amount it stands for.
    Play together
    Count the dots out loud before matching.
    Off-screen
    Write 1, 2 and 3 on paper and put that many raisins on each.
  • Find it · Patterns

    Pattern Train

    Continue AB, AAB and ABC patterns.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Finding the rule that repeats.
    Play together
    Clap the pattern: loud for one picture, soft for the other.
    Off-screen
    Make a necklace pattern with pasta and cereal hoops.
  • Find it · Phonics

    First Sounds

    Hear the first sound of a familiar word.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Phonological awareness: hearing sounds inside words.
    Play together
    Stretch the sound: “sssssun”. Say each picture name slowly.
    Off-screen
    Play “I spy something beginning with b”.
  • Sort · Sorting

    Sorting Station

    Group objects by color, shape or kind.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Choosing one rule and sticking to it.
    Play together
    Say the rule out loud before each round: “This time it’s colors.”
    Off-screen
    Sort the laundry by color together.
  • Put in order · Sequencing

    First, Next, Last

    Put three events in a sensible order.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Using “first, next, last”: the start of retelling a story.
    Play together
    Tell the story in your own words as each picture is chosen.
    Off-screen
    While making a snack, say: first we wash, next we cut, last we eat.
  • Memory · Memory

    Memory Pairs

    Turn cards over and remember where pairs are.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Short-term visual memory and taking turns.
    Play together
    Take turns with your child and say each picture out loud.
    Off-screen
    Put three toys on a tray, cover them, and remove one. What’s gone?
  • Find it · Comparing

    More or Fewer?

    Compare two groups and say which has more or fewer.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Comparing amounts, by looking or by counting.
    Play together
    Line them up in your head: which side runs out first?
    Off-screen
    Give two plates of grapes. Who has more?
  • Find it · Listening

    Story Time Questions

    Listen to a very short story and answer a question about it.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Listening, remembering and understanding a story.
    Play together
    Read the story yourself in a funny voice, then ask.
    Off-screen
    After a bedtime story, ask “Who was in the story?”
  • Touch and count · Counting

    Count to 10

    Count larger groups, from 6 towards 10.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Keeping track while counting more objects.
    Play together
    If a number is missed, count again together, slowly.
    Off-screen
    Count stairs as you climb them.
  • Match pairs · Letters

    Letter Friends

    Match a capital letter to a picture whose name starts with it.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Linking letter shapes to their sounds.
    Play together
    Say the letter sound, not just its name: “B says b”.
    Off-screen
    Find the first letter of your child’s name on cereal boxes and signs.
  • Find it · Sequences

    What’s Missing?

    Find the missing piece in a simple sequence.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Spotting order and reasoning about what fits.
    Play together
    Read the row out loud, and say “hmm” at the gap.
    Off-screen
    Line up 1, 2, 3, 4 blocks in towers. Hide one tower. Which is missing?
  • Find it · Position words

    Where Is the Ball?

    Understand above, below, beside, between, in and on.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Position words used in instructions and in math.
    Play together
    Use a real ball and box to try each one.
    Off-screen
    Play “Put teddy under the table, on the chair, beside the door.”
  • Find it · Rhyming

    Rhyme Time

    Hear words that rhyme.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Listening to the ends of words: an early reading skill.
    Play together
    Say both words together: “cat, hat”. Do they sound the same at the end?
    Off-screen
    Make up silly rhymes at dinner: “spoon, moon, balloon!”

Ages 4–6

Ready for School

Early reading and math, thinking puzzles, feelings and everyday routines. 35 activities, easiest first. Off-screen ideas for ages 4–6.

For grown-ups

  • Pick one strand at a time: reading, math, thinking or everyday skills.
  • Simplify: read the question aloud and point to each choice.
  • Stretch: after a game, ask your child to make up their own question.
  • Children develop at different paces. These games are practice, not a test.

Early literacy

  • Find it · Letter recognition

    Letter Spotter

    Recognize capital and lowercase letters.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Telling letter shapes apart, including tricky ones like b and d.
    Play together
    Trace the letter in the air together.
    Off-screen
    Find letters on food boxes in the kitchen.
  • Find it · Letter–sound relationships

    Letter Sounds

    Match a picture to the letter its name starts with.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Knowing the most common sound for each letter.
    Play together
    Say the word slowly and listen for the very first sound.
    Off-screen
    Choose a letter of the day and hunt for things that start with it.
  • Find it · Phonological awareness

    Clap the Beats

    Count the beats (syllables) in a word.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Breaking words into parts, which helps with reading and spelling.
    Play together
    Clap each beat together. Hands under the chin work too: the chin drops on each beat.
    Off-screen
    Clap the names of everyone in the family.
  • Find it · Vocabulary

    Word Groups

    Use category words: fruit, vegetable, vehicle, clothing, weather.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Organizing vocabulary into groups.
    Play together
    Ask “What else is a vehicle?”
    Off-screen
    Play a category game: name as many fruits as you can.
  • Find it · Beginning and ending sounds

    Sound Detective

    Hear the sound at the end of a word.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Phonological awareness beyond the first sound.
    Play together
    Say each word slowly and hold the last sound.
    Off-screen
    Clap when you hear a word that ends in “t”: hat, cat, sit…
  • Find it · Rhyming sounds

    Odd One Out: Rhymes

    Find the word that does not rhyme with the others.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Hearing word endings and comparing them.
    Play together
    Say all three names out loud, slowly.
    Off-screen
    Say three words, two that rhyme. Swap roles and let your child try.
  • Find it · Blending

    Sound Blender

    Blend separate sounds into a word.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    The core skill of decoding: pushing sounds together.
    Play together
    Say the sounds slowly, then faster and faster until the word pops out.
    Off-screen
    Robot talk at dinner: “Pass the c-u-p.”
  • Find it · Recognizing words

    Word Spotter

    Recognize common short words by sight.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Reading high-frequency words that appear in almost every book.
    Play together
    Point to the word in a real book afterwards.
    Off-screen
    Write “the” on sticky notes and hunt for it in a picture book.
  • Find it · Listening comprehension

    Listen Carefully

    Answer a question about a story read aloud.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Holding details in mind while listening.
    Play together
    Pause after the story and ask your child to retell it first.
    Off-screen
    At bedtime, stop a familiar story and ask “What happens next?”
  • Put in order · Sequencing and storytelling

    Story Builder

    Order four pictures, then tell the story.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Sequencing events and retelling with “first, then, next, finally”.
    Play together
    Once it is in order, ask your child to tell you the story.
    Off-screen
    Draw three pictures of your day and put them in order.
  • Find it · Reading comprehension

    Read and Find

    Read a short sentence and find the matching picture.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Reading for meaning, not just sounding out.
    Play together
    Let your child point to each word as you read together.
    Off-screen
    Write a short note like “Find the red cup” and let your child read and do it.

Early mathematics

  • Find it · Number recognition

    Number Finder

    Recognize written numbers to 20.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Reading two-digit numbers, including tricky teens.
    Play together
    Say “one-four makes fourteen” to show the tens and ones.
    Off-screen
    Spot numbers on houses, buses and clocks.
  • Touch and count · One-to-one correspondence

    Touch and Count

    Count up to 12 objects, touching each only once.

    For grown-ups
    Practising
    Accurate counting of larger groups.
    Play together
    Count with a pointing finger, never skipping or double-counting.
    Off-screen
    Count spoons as you set the table, one for each person.

    For grown-ups
    Practising

    Quick Look

    For grown-ups
    Practising

  • Find it · Shapes and spatial reasoning

  • Put in order · Ordering numbers

    Number Line-Up

  • Find it · Patterns and sequences

About these games

Ages are a rough guide. Children of the same age can be at very different points, so start wherever your child is comfortable and move up or down freely. The games were written to follow common early-years practice; they have not been independently reviewed by early-years specialists, and they do not replace parents, teachers or individual developmental advice.

Under 2, the American Academy of Pediatrics advises keeping screen use shared and brief. The Little World games are designed to be played together, on a lap.

Privacy: the games run in your browser. They do not record answers, need an account or send anything anywhere. The only thing remembered on this device is whether sound is on. Privacy.
