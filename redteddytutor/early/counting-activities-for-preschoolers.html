Counting · Free · No sign-up

Counting activities, from toddlers to kindergarten.

Reciting “one, two, three” is not the same as counting. Real counting means saying one number for each thing, and knowing the last number says how many. Children usually get there in steps, and touching each object helps more than anything else.

See 7 activities

What it often looks like by age

Ages 2–3
Saying number words, and counting two or three things with help.
Ages 3–4
Counting up to about five objects and answering “how many?” with the last number.
Ages 4–6
Counting up to 20 objects, comparing groups, and counting on from a number other than one.

Every child is different, and these are broad patterns, not milestones to test against.

Tips that help

  • Line objects up in a row. Scattered objects are much harder to count.
  • Count things that move: stairs, jumps, claps.
  • After counting, ask “so how many?” to connect the count to the total.

Counting

Count the dots

Ages 2–3On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 2 min

What it builds: Count up to three objects, touching each one once.

You need

  • Three blocks or crackers

How to play

  1. Play the on-screen counting game together.
  2. Line up three blocks and touch each one as you count aloud.
  3. Ask “How many?” and say the last number together.

Things to say

  • One, two, three. Three blocks!
  • Let’s touch each one.
  • How many crackers do you have?

Take it further

Count stairs out loud as you climb them.

Fine motor

Play dough rolls and pinches

Ages 2–3Off-screenAbout 15 min

What it builds: Strengthen the hand muscles later used for holding a pencil.

You need

  • Play dough
  • A blunt plastic knife or cookie cutter

How to play

  1. Roll dough into snakes and balls.
  2. Pinch small pieces off and press them flat.
  3. Count the balls you made together.

Things to say

  • Long snake! Short snake.
  • Squash it flat.
  • How many balls did you make?

Take it further

Press shapes into the dough with a cookie cutter and name them.

Counting

Count to five

Ages 3–4On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 2 min

What it builds: Count up to five objects and know the last number says how many.

You need

  • Five small toys

How to play

  1. Play the counting game together.
  2. Set out five toys and count them, touching each.
  3. Mix them up and count again. Still five!

Things to say

  • How many are there?
  • We moved them. Are there still five?
  • Show me three fingers.

Take it further

Count out five crackers at snack time.

Numbers

Match the number to the group

Ages 3–4On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 3 min

What it builds: Link a written numeral to a group of that many objects.

You need

  • Sticky notes with 1–5 written on them
  • Small toys

How to play

  1. Play the matching game together.
  2. Put a sticky note on the table and build a group of that many toys.
  3. Swap: your child builds a group and you find its number.

Things to say

  • This says three. Can you make three?
  • Which number matches?
  • Let’s count to check.

Take it further

Spot numbers on doors, buses and clocks.

Counting and cardinality

Counting up to ten

Ages 4–6On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 3 min

What it builds: Count up to ten objects, saying one number for each.

You need

  • Ten small objects
  • An egg carton

How to play

  1. Play the counting game.
  2. Put one object in each egg cup as you count.
  3. Ask how many, then how many more to fill ten.

Things to say

  • Ten! How did you know?
  • How many more to make ten?
  • Let’s count in twos.

Take it further

Count to 20, then to 100 by tens, while waiting in line.

Relates to Common Core: K.CC.B.4 K.CC.B.5

Comparing numbers

More or fewer?

Ages 4–6On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 3 min

What it builds: Compare two groups and say which has more, fewer or the same.

You need

  • Two plates
  • Raisins or blocks

How to play

  1. Play the sorting game.
  2. Put a different number on each plate and ask which has more.
  3. Line them up one-to-one to check.

Things to say

  • Which plate has more?
  • How can we be sure?
  • Now they’re the same. How did we do that?

Take it further

Play the card game War with cards 1 to 10.

Relates to Common Core: K.CC.C.6

Patterns

Counting in twos

Ages 4–6On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 3 min

What it builds: Continue a counting pattern and count on from a number other than 1.

You need

  • Pairs of socks

How to play

  1. Play the pattern game.
  2. Count pairs of socks by twos: 2, 4, 6.
  3. Start counting from 4 instead of 1.

Things to say

  • 2, 4, 6… what comes next?
  • Start at 5 and count on.
  • What number comes just before 9?

Take it further

Hop on a hopscotch grid while counting on.

Relates to Common Core: K.CC.A.2
