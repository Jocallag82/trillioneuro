Letter sounds · Free · No sign-up

Letter sounds: early phonics through play.

Reading starts with hearing sounds in spoken words, often before a child knows any letters. Playing with sounds out loud, then linking them to letters, builds the foundation kindergarten phonics depends on.

See 5 activities

What it often looks like by age

Ages 2–3
Noticing letters, especially the first letter of their own name.
Ages 3–4
Hearing the first sound in a word, like the /s/ in sun.
Ages 4–6
Matching sounds to letters and following print from left to right.

Every child is different, and these are broad patterns, not milestones to test against.

Tips that help

  • Say the sound, not the letter name: /m/, not “em”.
  • Keep sounds short and clean: /b/, not “buh”.
  • Start with sounds that can be stretched, like /s/, /m/ and /f/.

First letters

Find the letter B

Ages 2–3On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 2 min

What it builds: Notice that letters have shapes and names, starting with letters that matter to your child.

You need

  • Alphabet magnets or a book

How to play

  1. Play the on-screen game together.
  2. Find the first letter of your child’s name on cereal boxes and signs.
  3. Trace it with a finger.

Things to say

  • B, like ball and bear.
  • That’s your letter!
  • Can you find another B?

Take it further

Trace letters in a tray of rice or sand.

Phonological awareness

Listen for the sound

Ages 3–4On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 3 min

What it builds: Hear the first sound in a word, starting with /s/.

You need

  • A few objects: sock, spoon, cup, ball

How to play

  1. Play the listening game with sound on.
  2. Hold up objects and say each name, stretching the first sound: “sssock”.
  3. Ask which ones start with /s/.

Things to say

  • Sssun. Do you hear the snake sound?
  • Does cup start with /s/?
  • Can you think of another /s/ word?

Take it further

Play I Spy with sounds: “I spy something that starts with /b/.”

Phonics

Listen: first sound

Ages 4–6On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 3 min

What it builds: Hear the first sound of a spoken word and match it to its letter.

You need

  • Magnetic letters

How to play

  1. Play the listening game with sound on.
  2. Say a word and ask your child to find the letter it starts with.
  3. Let them choose a word for you.

Things to say

  • Mmmoon starts with /m/. Which letter makes /m/?
  • What sound does your name start with?
  • Can you find something that starts with /t/?

Take it further

Write the first letter of each family member’s name on a fridge list.

Relates to Common Core: RF.K.2.d RF.K.3.a

Letter sounds

What does it start with?

Ages 4–6On-screen game + off-screen playAbout 3 min

What it builds: Match pictured words to the letter for their first sound.

You need

  • Picture cards or a picture book

How to play

  1. Play the game together.
  2. Point to pictures in a book and say the first sound.
  3. Find the letter on the page.

Things to say

  • Ball. /b/ /b/ ball.
  • Which letter makes that sound?
  • What else starts with /b/?

Take it further

Make a letter book: one page per letter, with drawings.

Relates to Common Core: RF.K.3.a

Print awareness

Print walk

Ages 4–6Off-screenAbout 10 min

What it builds: Know that print carries meaning, is read left to right and is made of letters.

You need

  • Signs, labels and packaging around you

How to play

  1. Point to words on signs and packets and read them aloud.
  2. Run your finger under the word from left to right.
  3. Ask your child to find letters they know.

Things to say

  • This says STOP. How do you know?
  • Which way do we read?
  • Can you find a letter from your name?

Take it further

Make labels for things in your child’s room together.

Relates to Common Core: RF.K.1.a RF.K.1.d
