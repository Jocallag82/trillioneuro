First words · Free · No sign-up

First words and animal sounds, together.

Toddlers learn words from people, not screens: from back-and-forth talk about the things in front of them. Animal sounds are often among the first “words”, because they are fun, repeatable and easy to copy.

See 5 activities

What it often looks like by age

Ages 1–2
Understanding many more words than they say. Pointing, babbling and animal sounds all count.
Ages 2–3
Word spurts, two-word phrases, and words used in pretend play.

Every child is different, and these are broad patterns, not milestones to test against.

Tips that help

  • Wait. Leave a pause after you talk so your child can take a turn.
  • Follow their pointing and name what they are interested in.
  • If you have concerns about your child’s speech, talk to your pediatrician.

First words activities to do together

Everyday words

Naming walk around the house

Ages 1–2Off-screenAbout 5 min

What it builds: Build vocabulary by hearing the names of real, touchable things.

You need

  • Nothing, just your home

How to play

  1. Carry your child around one room.
  2. Stop at five things and name them, letting your child touch each one.
  3. Repeat the same walk on other days. Familiar words stick.

Things to say

  • Door. Knock knock!
  • This is the light. On… off.
  • Soft blanket.

Take it further

Add an action word: “open the door”, “turn on the light”.

Animals and sounds

Animal sounds game

Ages 1–2Off-screenAbout 5 min

What it builds: Copy sounds and link them to animals: an early step in speech and listening.

You need

  • A picture book with animals, or toy animals

How to play

  1. Show one animal at a time and make its sound.
  2. Pause, smile and wait. Many children try the sound themselves.
  3. Make the sound again, a little louder or softer, and see if they copy.

Things to say

  • The dog says woof woof.
  • Can you say baa like the sheep?
  • Quack! Where did the duck go?

Take it further

Sing “Old MacDonald Had a Farm” and pause before each sound.

Storytelling

Point and say storytime

Ages 1–2Off-screenAbout 5 min

What it builds: Enjoy books together and learn that pictures have names.

You need

  • A sturdy board book

How to play

  1. Let your child turn the pages, even out of order.
  2. Point to one thing per page and name it.
  3. Follow what they point at and talk about that instead.

Things to say

  • Look, a bear! Like Red Teddy.
  • What’s that?
  • The bear is sleeping. Shh.

Take it further

Make a small photo book of family members and name each person.

First words

Look and Name cards

Ages 1–2On-screen, togetherAbout 2 min

What it builds: Hear and connect everyday words to pictures: colors, animals and familiar things.

You need

  • A phone or tablet, on your lap together

How to play

  1. Open the cards and hold the screen where you can both see it.
  2. Point to the picture and name it slowly: “A duck!”
  3. Tap the card to hear the word and the sound, then wait for your child to look, point or babble.
  4. After six cards, put the screen away and find one of the things for real.

Things to say

  • Where is the duck?
  • What does the cow say? Moo!
  • Your ball is red too.

Take it further

Find one thing from the cards in your home and give it to your child to hold.

Imaginative play and language

Teddy’s picnic

Ages 2–3Off-screenAbout 15 min

What it builds: Use words for food, sharing and feelings in pretend play.

You need

  • A soft toy or teddy
  • Toy or real cups and plates

How to play

  1. Set up a picnic for teddy.
  2. Ask your child to give teddy a cup, a plate, one snack.
  3. Talk about how teddy feels: hungry, full, happy, sleepy.

Things to say

  • Teddy is hungry. What shall we give him?
  • One for you, one for teddy.
  • Is teddy happy now?

Take it further

Add a second toy and practice sharing fairly.
