How to play Times Table Meteors

  1. Pick which times tables to practise: 2, 5 and 10 first, then 3, 4 and 6, then 7, 8 and 9.
  2. The glowing meteor is closest to the ground. Tap its answer to blast it.
  3. Answer quickly to build a combo: every 3 in a row multiplies your points, up to five times.
  4. A wrong answer pulls the meteor closer, and three meteors landing ends the game. At the end you see which facts to practise next.

Why this multiplication game works

Times tables stick through quick retrieval practice: recalling the fact, not reading it. Meteors give gentle time pressure that builds speed, and the four answer choices are deliberately tricky. They include mistakes children really make, such as adding instead of multiplying (6 + 7 = 13) or being one group off (7 × 7 = 49 when the question was 6 × 7).

Start with the 2s, 5s and 10s, which follow easy counting patterns. Then move to 3s, 4s and 6s, and leave the 7s, 8s and 9s until last. The end screen lists the exact facts your child missed, so practice time goes where it is needed.

Skills and standards

  • Fluently multiply within 100 (Common Core 3.OA.C.7)
  • Multiplication as equal groups and commutativity: 3 × 8 = 8 × 3 (3.OA.B.5)
  • Times tables to 12 × 12 for grades 4–5 fluency and the UK multiplication tables check

Tips for parents and teachers

Ten minutes a day beats an hour on Sunday. Play two or three rounds, then say the missed facts aloud together. Once your child reaches 300 or more on a level, move up.

Frequently asked questions

What age is this multiplication game for?

Ages 7 to 11 (grades 2–5). Younger children can start with the 2, 5 and 10 level.

Is it really free?

Yes. There is no sign-up, no ads, and nothing to download. Best scores are saved only on your device.

Does it work on iPad and Chromebook?

Yes. It runs in any modern browser and is built for touch screens.

How do I learn times tables fast?

Short daily practice of the facts you miss. The game lists those facts after every round so you can focus on them.
