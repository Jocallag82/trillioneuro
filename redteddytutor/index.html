Try it here

A real question, from the real engine.

Pick a grade band. This is the same question generator your child uses in the app, so every question is new. Get one wrong on purpose and see what happens.

  1. Help before they answer. A hint, or “Show me how”: a similar question worked step by step with its own numbers, in words for their age. Then they try theirs.
  2. A wrong answer gets a hint, not the answer. Two hints, then a gentle reveal. Nothing is taken away.
  3. Many wrong answers carry a reason. Choosing 7 when there are 6 dots usually means one was counted twice, and the hint says how to avoid that.
  4. In the app, every first try is recorded on your device, and that decides what comes next.

How a session works

A short session, chosen for one child.

No mystery box. Each step follows a rule you can read, and the app says why each activity was picked.

  1. 1

    A quick check finds the start

    Up to seven questions per subject, about two minutes. It steps up after a right answer and down after a wrong one, and no marks are shown. Or skip it and start at the usual level for the age.

  2. 2

    Today’s plan picks four things

    A review that is due comes first, then a mix-up worth fixing, then new skills whose foundations are already in place. A new skill never comes before the skills it builds on.

  3. 3

    A lesson, then practice that builds

    A new skill opens with a short lesson and a worked example. Practice then starts easier and builds up: typed answers, number lines, ten frames, triangles, code to trace and passages to read. Not just tapping A, B, C or D.

  4. 4

    Skills come back before they fade

    Once a skill is going well, it comes back for review after 1 day, then 3, 7, 14, 30 and 60 days. A shaky skill comes back as practice, with easier questions first.

Ask Red Teddy

A tutor that helps them think.

Inside any question, or in its own Ask Red Teddy space for any topic, your child can ask for a simpler explanation, another example or a practice question. Red Teddy is built to teach, not to do the homework. Available for signed-in families, ages 5 and up.

  • Marking is always done by the app’s own rules. Red Teddy never decides whether an answer is right.
  • Explanations match the learner’s level: simple for young children, precise and grown-up for high schoolers.
  • A reply that gives the answer away, contains a link or looks wrong is dropped. The written hint is shown instead.
  • Unsafe messages get a fixed, kind reply and never reach the AI model.
  • Messages are not stored. Use is limited per family per day, shared by all children on the account.
  • If Teddy is unavailable, every activity still works with its written hints.

For parents

See what was practised, and how sure we are.

The parent view is built only from answers your child actually gave. It keeps apart four things that most apps blur together.

Open the parent view
Measured
Activities finished, answers given, right first time or after a hint, and activity week by week.
Inferred, with confidence
Which skills look secure, which need support, and which mistakes keep coming back. A skill is only called secure after enough recent answers given without help.
Assumed
Earlier skills that the quick check stepped past are marked as assumed, not as learned.
Next
What the plan will suggest, and why, in plain words. For example: “Last practised 8 days ago. A quick review helps it stick.”

It does not give your child a grade, compare them with other children or predict exam results. You can delete the recorded progress at any time.

Safety and privacy

Built for children, so kept small.

Read the privacy notice

  • No account needed to start. Without one, progress stays in this browser only.
  • Parents sign in, not children. Sign-in is by an email link. A child is a nickname and an age range, never a surname, birthday, school or photo.
  • No chat between users. No public profiles, no feeds and nothing children can post.
  • No adverts and no ad trackers.
  • No streak pressure. Missing a day costs nothing. A wrong answer changes what comes next, never a score.
  • Sound only after a tap. Every spoken line is also written on screen. Animation follows the device’s reduced-motion setting.

Questions parents ask

Before you start.

Is it really free?

Yes. Everything for ages 1 to kindergarten on the early-years pages is free with no sign-up, and that will not change. Every activity, the game, the plan, review and the parent view are free. The only planned paid feature is more daily help from Teddy, and it is not on sale yet.

Which grade should I pick?

Your child’s school grade. It sets the starting point, the curriculum shown and how the screens look. The quick check then moves up or down, and you can change the grade at any time.

Is it aligned to Common Core?

Where a skill relates to a Common Core math or English standard, or an NGSS science standard, we show the code and whether our practice covers it fully or in part. Many skills cover part of a standard, because standards also expect things like written explanations that practice questions cannot check. States adopt standards differently, and we have not yet checked any state’s own version. Social studies and computer science standards are set by states and are not mapped yet.

Which grades and subjects are covered?

Kindergarten to grade 12. Math and English go deepest, from counting and phonics to algebra, trigonometry, derivatives and literary analysis. Science, social studies and computer science have a smaller set of skills that is growing. The curriculum page shows exactly what exists at each grade, and what does not yet.

Can a young child use it alone?

Not at the youngest ages, and that is deliberate. Little World (ages 1–2) is for doing together and is mostly off-screen. Ages 2 to 5 are designed for use with a grown-up nearby: instructions can be read aloud, and wherever possible the question is carried by pictures.

What happens to my child’s answers?

Without an account they stay in your browser. If you sign in, they are saved to your family account so you can use more than one device, and only your account can read them. You can delete them from the parent view.

Does it work on a shared tablet?

Yes. When a parent signs in, each child gets their own profile. Without signing in, everyone using that browser shares one set of progress.
